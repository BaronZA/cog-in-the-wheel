SL33P13-773P ("Sleepie") — Pentadrone
HP 32 · AC 16 · 5×Arm +4 5(1d6+2) · Paralysis Gas 30ft cone DC11 Con
  • Thinks party are its decaton superiors. Follows orders to the letter.
  • Developing individual consciousness — starts asking why.
  • Outranks all modrons except the decaton — can dismiss patrols, escort party.
  • Part 3: too confused to act. Broken modrons ignore it.
  • Part 5: tridrone threatens decommissioning → sides with party, uses Aid each round.
  • Brought to Melvaunt: story award "Friendship of SL33P13-773P".
Roleplay Notes
  • Speaks in clipped, declarative sentences. No contractions. Always addresses party as "Superior" or "Decaton-class designate."
  • When asked why it's helping: it doesn't understand the question. It follows lawful hierarchy. The party outranks it. That's sufficient.
  • Slowly start slipping in questions mid-combat or mid-march: "Query: what is the purpose of this action?" Then seem confused it asked.
  • If it takes damage, it reports damage like a system log: "Warning: structural integrity at 60%. Continuing."
  • Part 5 pivot: when the tridrone says decommissioning, play a beat of silence — then Sleepie steps between the tridrone and the party: "Negative. These units have demonstrated superior function."

Black — Human Envoy of the Cult
Cult Fanatic · HP 33 · AC 13 · 2×Dagger +4 · Spells: guiding bolt, hold person, spiritual weapon
  • Pale, gaunt, short black hair, goatee, yellow snake eyes. Wears all black.
  • Polite but openly disdainful. Gives only his name and "I am a friend of the modrons."
  • Won't risk his life against unknowns who got in unopposed — too unpredictable.
  • Goal: delay party until the decaton arrives. Then escape however he can.
  • Will jump from a window if cornered — may die but he knows it triggers the alarm.
Roleplay Notes
  • Never raises his voice. Slow, deliberate speech. Treat every sentence like he's slightly bored by having to explain things to children.
  • He's genuinely not afraid of the party — he's afraid of the decaton, and he knows it's coming.
  • He will not negotiate, bargain, or give information. He'll deflect with pleasantries: "An interesting question. I prefer not to answer it."
  • If the party attacks without warning: "Ah. Direct. I respect that, at least." — then he fights tactically, not desperately.
  • If cornered near a window, have him glance at it. Give the players a moment to act. Then he smiles and steps through.
  • He knows the decaton is minutes away. His entire demeanor is someone killing time.

    Session 1 — Melvaunt Nights

    Last time, outside the Winking Narwhal, a drunken brawl broke out in the alley. Caleb stepped forward to break it up while Drake used prestidigitation as a distraction — and while nobody was looking, Krisys quietly lifted a coin purse from the chaos with mage hand.

    The brawlers scattered. Then the clock struck eight bells, and a scream cut through the night.

    You found a dockworker face-down in an alley, a dagger buried to the hilt in his back. Caleb charged in and somehow avoided the scattered caltrops — the swarms came alive anyway. The creatures were brutally hard to hit at first, their chaos-binding making them resistant to almost everything. When each swarm was beaten below half, something gave way and they became easier to damage. You put them all down.

    Gardak found a folded note pinned under the dagger and passed it quietly to Drake. Then Sergeant Eledstra arrived with city guards — and Drake immediately hid the note. Tav cooperated openly with her and earned her trust. Drake tried to fake-hand over a different letter and got caught. Eledstra's opinion of him dropped sharply.

    Even so, the party's overall cooperation earned you two potions of healing, which went to Tav. You worked out the puzzle together — ADAMANTINE BLACKSMITH — and split from Eledstra, racing toward the marketplace.

    You searched the market but the rolls were unkind. You only found Boltac's stall just as his own Gondsmen constructs animated around him. The first round they stood still — just long enough to close in.

    Boltac grabbed a weapon and fought alongside you. Drake made a bold call: he ran into the middle of four Gondsmen and unleashed Arms of Hadar. The resistance and saves meant most of the damage bounced — and the spell caught Elly and Tav in the blast too. Elly was furious and refused to heal Drake. Tav dropped to 1 HP before Elly brought him back to full. On the last Gondsman, the party pulled their punches and knocked it out non-lethally — that earned Elly back.

    Then, as the last construct fell, a crossbow bolt came from the rooftops — aimed at Boltac. Drake was standing right next to the dwarf. He took the bolt instead and dropped to zero. Caleb pulled him back from the edge. Elly, still irritated but not heartless, healed him further.

    Drake pulled the bolt free and handed it to Elly, who noticed the shaft was hollow. They snapped off the tip: inside was Puzzle 2. TEMPLE OF LOVIATAR. TEN HOURS. HALF ELF.

    You shouted to Boltac and Elly to send word to the Watch. Krisys convinced the city guards along the route to relay the message ahead. And then you ran.

    Scene — Drake Stays Behind Drake slows to a stop, one hand pressed to the wound in his arm where the bolt struck. His breathing is labored. "Go," he grits through his teeth. "I'll... I'll catch up. Someone needs to make sure Boltac and Elly get word to the Watch anyway." He doesn't wait for an argument — he's already limping back toward the marketplace.
    Session 2 — The Temple & the Chase

    You arrived at the Temple of Loviatar to find it shuttered and dark — though candlelight still flickered behind the doors. What followed was a masterclass in overthinking a simple problem. Gardak approached the entrance and inspected what turned out to be a perfectly ordinary door, then climbed up to examine the speaking grate set beside it before the party arrived at the revolutionary conclusion: knock.

    The priestess Zellani answered. She was skeptical, but you explained the night's events and produced the writ from the city watch. That was enough to get you inside.

    She hadn't quite finished doubting that anyone would target her temple when the gongs began to sound. The party took up positions without hesitation — Krisys ordered Zellani behind you all. Then the shadows moved, and the chaos fiends came. Zellani called forth her imps to hold the flanks.

    Tav led the charge, blurring forward and carving through the first fiend so thoroughly it barely had time to register the threat. He celebrated a half-second too soon: a chaos bolt caught him square in the chest, dropping him to death's door in a manner that was, by all accounts, anatomically creative. After a quick heal the party cleaned up the rest.

    In the silence that followed, Zellani threw her head back and addressed the heavens directly:

    Read aloud — Zellani "You see!" she shouts at the heavens. "You thought to bring me death and instead I have brought you exquisite pain. Now show yourself and feel the sting of my whip!"
    Read aloud — the answer From above the altar, a quiet voice whispers two words: "Very well." The glass skylight that arches over the sanctuary shatters. A rain of broken glass cascades down upon you.

    Zellani cries out in pain and drops to one knee — a massive shard has impaled her shoulder. Floating above you in the wreckage of the once-beautiful window is a spindly, cloaked figure. You cannot make out its face or features, but its arms and legs are very thin. It carries a bow, nocked with a single black arrow, aimed at the heart of the priestess of Loviatar.

    There was little time. Caleb stepped between the arrow and Zellani, shield raised, and turned the shot — deflected cleanly to the marble floor, though the sheer force of it knocked him off his feet. The assassin swept back up through the broken skylight and into the night sky, its movement labored.

    Read aloud — Zellani Through clenched teeth, the priestess says: "Door… to the roof. Behind the curtain. Hurry. Get that bastard for me."

    On the roof, the chase began. As you leapt between rooftops, chaos rifts tore open across your path — each one an obstacle, each one overcome. Tav set the pace, as monks do, pulling steadily ahead of the field.

    You caught the assassin at the Finger of the Gods lighthouse. It turned to face you, and you finally saw it clearly:

    Read aloud — the assassin revealed A square, cubical face, with swirling eyes and metallic skin. No — not just a face. The creature's entire body is essentially a perfect cube, about the size of a man's torso. It has no neck or head; its face peers at you from the front of the cube. It is bipedal, but its arms and legs are skinny and elongated. It moves with a surprising quickness for something so strangely proportioned.
    Read aloud — the portal You hear the familiar sound of the clock tower striking. But the noise isn't coming from somewhere back in the city — it's coming from directly in front of you. From the lighthouse.

    A massive portal, easily fifteen feet in diameter, opens up in the side of the Finger of the Gods. Through it you see a realm of clockwork gears, all interlocking, all moving in time, performing a calculation so vast and so complicated that not even the minds of the gods can fathom its purpose.

    The modron... the machine... the murderer... bows slightly in your direction and turns to enter the portal.

    You all attempt to stop him, and finally hit the assassin causing it to stumble into the portal as it closes, sheering off its arm.

    You pick up the metallic arm and take it to Eledestra, and you're left with many questions and precious few answers
